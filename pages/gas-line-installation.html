Why licensing & permits matter

Unlicensed gas work isn't a shortcut — it's a liability.

Unpermitted gas work in Massachusetts isn't just unsafe; it can invalidate your homeowner's insurance, hold up a closing during resale, and (in the worst case) put first responders into a leak situation they didn't know about.

Every line we run goes in under Phil's Massachusetts Master Plumber and gas-fitter license, with the permit pulled at the town hall before we touch a fitting. Inspection passed, paperwork on file.

  • Insurance coverageMost homeowner policies exclude losses from unpermitted gas work. A small fire becomes a denied claim.
  • Property resaleBuyer's inspector flags unpermitted gas almost every time. Closings stall while it's redone correctly.
  • Code & safetyMA gas code exists because of fires and explosions that happened. Pressure tests, leak checks, proper sizing — non-negotiable.
  • Resale documentationWe hand you the permit number and final inspection sign-off. Keep it with your home records.

Safety & code compliance

Every line: sized, sealed, soap-tested, inspected.

We don't take shortcuts on gas work. Every joint gets a leak check. Every line gets a pressure test. Every job ends with a state inspector signing off.

  • ✓ Black iron, CSST, or copper sized to the appliance load
  • ✓ Gas-rated sealant on every threaded joint
  • ✓ Pressure tested above working pressure for the required hold time
  • ✓ Drip leg / sediment trap at every appliance
  • ✓ Shut-off valve installed within 6 ft of each appliance
